## Endless Opportunities Story Arc

This is the shared narrative backbone for the five-week course. Use it when updating any week so the AJ/Nia/Malik plot stays continuous even if different agents work on different lessons in parallel.

### Core cast

- `AJ`: smooth, social, instinctive, spots the opening first
- `Nia`: sharp, skeptical, keeps pressing for the real logic
- `Malik`: technical, observant, turns insight into system and build choices
- `Sage`: warm story guide voice, not a classroom lecturer

### Tone rules

- The crew is Black, smart, current, and confident without sounding fake or over-written.
- Sage narrates with flow and clarity, but does not over-explain obvious things.
- The story should feel like an origin-to-builder arc, not five unrelated lessons.
- Do not narrate section titles. Narration should drop straight into scene context.

### Throughline

- Week 0: the crew learns to see problems as opportunity maps
- Week 1: the crew learns that shallow answers hide the real problem
- Week 2: the crew gathers proof from data, signals, and contradictions
- Week 3: the crew turns clear thinking into a first working tool
- Week 4: the crew tests, sharpens, and presents a real solution

### Week beats

#### Week 0: The Game We Live In

- AJ notices everyday frustration and sees that problems are everywhere.
- Nia reframes complaints as clues.
- Malik tracks where AI changes the leverage.
- End state: the world no longer looks random. It looks solvable.

#### Week 1: The Art of Questions

- The crew starts with a surface answer that sounds fine at first.
- Nia keeps asking why until the original framing breaks.
- AJ learns that confidence is not the same as understanding.
- Malik maps dependencies and sees how the system actually works.
- End state: the crew moves from opinion to investigation.

#### Week 2: Data

- AJ starts spotting useful signals in posts, reviews, comments, and public info.
- Nia compares what people claim with what they actually do.
- Malik organizes evidence into patterns and gaps.
- End state: the crew moves from intuition to proof.

#### Week 3: Building

- AJ names what should get built and who it should help.
- Nia forces the prompts and requirements to get specific.
- Malik tests the early versions and pushes iteration.
- End state: the crew moves from intimidation to agency.

#### Week 4: Portfolio / Presentation

- AJ chooses the story worth presenting.
- Nia keeps the explanation honest and rooted in the actual problem.
- Malik shows the build, the workflow, and the evidence of learning.
- End state: the crew is not just learning tools. They are problem solvers now.

### Page structure target for every week

For each story segment on a lesson page:

1. Storyboard-first narrated slide deck
2. Three activity sets:
   - comprehension
   - application
   - synthesis

### Visual and UX rules

- Use week 0 as the style baseline.
- Keep Sage hard-forced unless there is an explicit lesson-level reason not to.
- Remove student-facing voice choosers, mute toggles, and extra settings controls.
- Keep the page visually clean: storyboard stage, slide dots, one play button, then activities.
- On mobile, match the workshop portal feel: `16:9` media frame, centered `cover`, minimal chrome.

### Validation checklist

- Story text matches the AJ/Nia/Malik arc for that week
- Narration does not read section titles
- No background music unless explicitly requested
- Activities match the narrated story facts
- Storyboard images are wired to narration and mobile-safe
